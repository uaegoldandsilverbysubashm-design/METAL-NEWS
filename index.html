Big monthly releases and how metals usually react

ReleaseWhen it usually landsIf hotter / strongerGoldSilverPlatinumCopper

Reactions describe the common first move, driven mostly by the US dollar and Treasury yields for gold and silver, and by growth expectations for platinum and copper. Markets price expectations in advance, so a result in line with the forecast often moves little, and a move can reverse within hours. Inflation releases (CPI, PCE) are the most disputed rows: a hot print can lift gold as an inflation hedge or hit it through higher yields, and the yield effect has usually won on the day. Treat this as a reading aid, not a trade signal.

Releases and consensus forecasts come from a free, unofficial weekly calendar feed, cached for about 15 minutes. If it is unreachable the page falls back to a short built-in list. Confirm exact dates and times on the BLS, Federal Reserve and BEA sites before relying on them. Actual results are not in the feed, so type them in yourself. Gold and silver spot trade around the clock on weekdays and are closed from Friday night to Sunday night. Session bands use summer-time hours (London and New York shift an hour later in Dubai time once daylight saving ends in late October and early November).
